## What AssetLink is

One-liner, word for word: **AssetLink is a decision intelligence platform for wealth management that finds the right
advisors and firms for your criteria, shows why they fit, and signals when to act.**

Tagline: **Know who, why and when.**

About (50 words): AssetLink helps wealth firms, asset managers and fintechs decide who to recruit, sell to or acquire.
It finds the right advisors and firms for each client's criteria, shows why they fit, signals when to act, and drafts
the outreach. Customers span insurance, asset management and wealth management.

Three jobs on the same data, always in this order. Lead with recruiting:

| Job | Their problem | What we say |
|---|---|---|
| Recruit advisors | "We hear about moves after our competitors have already called." Contact data is stale. | Find advisors who fit your profile, and reach them when they're ready to move. |
| Sell to advisors | Wholesalers spend hours on data packs and CRM notes instead of talking to advisors. | Put your product in front of the advisors most likely to need it. |
| Acquire firms | You can't see which firms are growing or in play until it's announced. | See which RIAs and wealth firms are growing, shrinking or in play. |

Buyers: Head of Advisor Recruiting, Chief Growth Officer, Head of Distribution or Sales Enablement, Corporate
Development, at US wealth firms (wirehouses, broker-dealers, RIA platforms, asset managers' sales teams).

The pain in their words (use these for hooks): "We hear about moves after our competitors have already called."
"Half our contact data bounces." "We have the list, but not enough people to work it." "Our lists don't reflect what a
good advisor looks like for us." "We keep calling people we've already ruled out." "We juggle five tabs and three
spreadsheets to build one list."

## What AssetLink is not

- Not a broker-dealer, custodian or RIA. It gives no investment advice, executes no trades, files nothing.
- Not a CRM replacement. It works alongside the systems a firm already has.
- Not a static advisor database or directory ("Decisions, not directories").
- Not autonomous. It drafts; people review and send every message. Nothing goes out on its own.
- Not a prediction engine. It signals who may be moving. The team decides who to call.
- US only.

## Voice

Authoritative, analytical, decisive, client-centric. Professional, sharp, confident, practical. Peer to peer: the
viewer is a senior recruiting or distribution leader who already pays for advisor data and feels its gaps.

- Speak to "you". Short, high-impact sentences. "Speed matters. Precision wins."
- State the buyer's problem in their words first, then the fix. Never a feature list.
- No hype without proof, no vague verbs ("improve your processes"), no excitement ("super excited", "game changer").
- "May" matters: "who may be moving", "who may be ready to move". Never certainty about an advisor's intent.
- The skill's house rules still bind: no em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y", no self-praise.
- VO casting: US market, a measured, senior, credible read. Calm pace (about 2.4 words per second). No hard sell.

Calls to action: "Book a demo" (default, as on shipped posts). "Book a 20-minute demo and we'll build a live list for
your team." "See it on your own criteria." "Get my list." "Compare a list." "Talk to sales" (enterprise).
End-card destination: `assetlink.ai`. Contact: `info@assetlink.ai`.

## Look

Sampled from the one-pager and the 24 Oct social post (see the comment in `theme.ts`):

- Light world: white and `#FBFBFD`, navy ink `#151A38`, lavender cards `#F2F4FF` with a `#CDC9E2` hairline.
- Brand purple `#5B49D3` for fills, rules above stats, labels in caps with wide tracking.
- Blue `#3F7FF9` as the one highlight: small caps labels, the button word, a key word. Never body text on white.
- Dark world: navy `#151A38`, or the left-to-right gradient near-black to `#3533CC` (`NIGHT_GRADIENT`) with white type.
  It suits the "static list" problem half turning into the "signals" half: the post's own structure is
  "FROM STATIC TO SIGNALS".
- Mark gradient violet `#7446FF` to blue `#22A4F6`: the mark, and at most one hero graphic per film.
- Type (provisional, identified by eye): heavy geometric sans headlines (Raleway 800), one serif line per scene for
  the subline or tagline (`SERIF`, Source Serif 4), neutral sans body (Inter). Numbers set in Raleway need `LINING`.
- Small radii, flat cards, thin borders, generous white space. Calm motion: no bounce, no overshoot.

## Product UI and demo data

- No screenshots of the current build are on hand. Before any scene that shows the product, ask for screenshots (gate
  2) and rebuild from them. Until then show the idea (a list, a profile card, a signal, a draft email) as plain cards
  in the brand's style, never as a pretend screenshot of a screen that may not exist.
- The safe demo path, from the positioning document: set a profile, show a list built to it, open one advisor, show a
  signal (a leaderboard, hotspot or recent change), draft an email into the drafts folder. Never show sorting, an
  open question to the AI, saved searches or watchlists.
- All advisors, firms, cities' office names, emails and numbers on screen are fictional and plausible. No real
  advisor, no real CRD number, no real firm, no real contact detail. This is a regulated industry.
- Name Outlook, Gmail and HubSpot in plain text only: never their logos, colours or interface.
- Never show a score, rank or percentage next to an advisor. A fit is shown as reasons ("CFP, 12 years at one firm,
  Northeast"), not as a number.
