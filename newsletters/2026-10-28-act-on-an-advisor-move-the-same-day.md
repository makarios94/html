# Your Best Recruit Was Ready on Monday. You Called on Friday.
*What happens to an advisor move in the week your team waits, and why noon on day one beats "this week."*

"Good find. Let's reach out once we've pulled the full picture."

You've said it in a pipeline meeting. It sounds responsible. It sounds like the opposite of spam.

Then the week happens. A comp review. Two candidate dinners. A branch manager who needs a call back. By Friday, the advisor you flagged on Monday is still a row in a spreadsheet.

The advisor isn't waiting for you.

## Careful and slow look the same from the outside

Teams rarely wait because they're lazy. They wait because they want to get it right.

But the advisor can't see your diligence. They only see who reached out, when, and whether the message showed any thought. A thoughtful message on day six reads a lot like silence on days one through five.

## The week, seen from the advisor's side

Picture a two-person team in Columbus. Solid book, mostly retirees and small-business owners. They've started to think seriously about leaving.

- **Monday:** They're open, curious and a little anxious. Their phone is quiet.
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** The first recruiters call. One mentions their client base by name.
- **Thursday:** Two intro meetings are on the calendar.
- **Friday:** Your email arrives. It's well researched. It's also the fourth one this week.

You didn't lose because your firm was the wrong fit. You lost because you showed up after they'd picked who to talk to.

And it never shows up on a dashboard. It just looks like a quiet quarter.

## Why noon, and not "this week"

Early in a move, an advisor is deciding who to talk to. A few days later, they're choosing between firms they've already talked to. You want to be in the first conversation, not the comparison.

A noon deadline forces a useful trade. You can't research everything by lunch, so you research the one thing that matters: can you say, in one sentence, why this advisor fits?

*"Your team's focus on [client type] in [City] is the market we're building in."*

If you can write that sentence, send the message. Everything else can wait for the first meeting. That's what the first meeting is for.

## The real cost of getting ready

Look back at that Columbus team. Your firm may have been their best option all along. Nothing on your side was wrong except the date.
