# Your Best Recruit Was Ready on Monday. You Called on Friday.
*What happens to an advisor move in the week your team waits, and an hour-by-hour routine for reaching them by noon on day one.*

"Good find. Let's reach out once we've pulled the full picture."

You've said it in a pipeline meeting. Most careful recruiting leaders have. It sounds responsible. It sounds like the opposite of spam.

Then the week happens. A comp review on Tuesday. Two candidate dinners. A branch manager who needs a call back. By Friday, the advisor you flagged on Monday is still a row in a spreadsheet.

The advisor isn't waiting for you. They've already taken two calls, and neither one came from your team.

## Careful and slow look the same from the outside

Teams rarely wait because they're lazy. They wait because they want to get it right. Nobody wants to call a strong advisor with a generic pitch.

But the advisor can't see your diligence. They only see who reached out, when, and whether the message showed any thought.

A thoughtful message on day six reads a lot like silence on days one through five.

So the question isn't whether to do the homework. It's whether the homework can fit inside one morning.

## The week, seen from the advisor's side

Picture a two-person team in Columbus. Solid book, mostly retirees and small-business owners. They've started to think seriously about leaving.

- **Monday:** They're open, curious and a little anxious. Their phone is quiet.
- **Tuesday and Wednesday:** The first recruiters call. Most pitch the platform. One mentions their client base by name.
- **Thursday:** Two intro meetings are on the calendar. Their time and attention are filling up.
- **Friday:** Your email arrives. It's polite and well researched. It's also the fourth one this week.

You didn't lose because your firm was the wrong fit. You lost because you showed up after they'd picked who to talk to.

Now run it across a month. Say your team spots ten moves worth chasing and reaches most of them a week late. You aren't competing for those advisors. You're applying to be the backup option.

For a Chief Growth Officer, that's the expensive part. Losing a great recruit to a slow week never shows up on a dashboard. It just looks like a quiet quarter.

## Why noon, and not "this week"

Early in a move, an advisor is deciding who to talk to. A few days later, they're choosing between firms they've already talked to. You want to be in the first conversation, not the comparison.

A noon deadline also does something useful to your process. It forces a trade. You can't research everything by lunch, so you research the one thing that matters: does this advisor fit, and can you say why?

## The same-day timeline

Here's the routine. It works with whatever tools you have today.

1. **7:30–8:00. Check who changed overnight.** Scan for advisor moves, team changes and firms in flux. Write down names only. No research yet.
2. **8:00–8:30. Cut against your profile.** Hold each name against your written profile of a good hire: client base, market, team size, book type. If they don't match, they're out. Most names should drop here.
3. **8:30–10:00. Write one fit sentence per name.** Then confirm a phone number and email that won't bounce. If you can't write the sentence, the advisor isn't a call today.
4. **10:00–11:30. Draft the first message.** Three parts: the fit sentence, one thing your firm does for teams like theirs, one easy question. Keep it under 90 words, and have a second person read it.
5. **By 12:00. Send it.** Don't schedule it for tomorrow. A good message today beats a better one on Friday.
6. **4:00. Log it and book the follow-up.** Put day three and day seven on the calendar, with a note on what you'll add each time.

The fit sentence carries the whole morning. A strong one sounds like this:

*"Your team's focus on [client type] in [City] is the market we're building in, and we've set up [resource] for teams your size."*

One sentence. Specific to them. Impossible to send to the other nine names on your list.

## What you stop doing

Same-day doesn't mean sloppy. It means you stop doing the research the advisor will never see.

The full career history. The 20-slide data pack. The third pass through LinkedIn. Week-later research tries to answer every question. Same-day research answers one: why should this advisor take my call?

Everything else can wait for the first meeting. That's what the first meeting is for.

## The real cost of getting ready

Look back at that Columbus team. Your firm may have been their best option all along. Nothing on your side was wrong except the date.
